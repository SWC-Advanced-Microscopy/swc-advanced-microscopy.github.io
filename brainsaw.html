What is serial-section two-photon microscopy?

Block-face serial two-photon tomography (STPT) provides high-resolution, whole-organ volumetric imaging whilst avoiding complex sample preparation. In an STPT system, a multi-photon microscope is coupled to a vibratome and a three-axis stage. Fixed tissue is embedded in agarose and mounted in a water bath riding on the stage. The tissue surface is exposed by the vibratome and imaged with a tile-scan by the 2-photon microscope. The STPT system alternates between slicing and imaging of the block face until all of the sample has been acquired.

STPT is ideal for reliable high-throughput whole-brain imaging; we have acquired about 8,000 brains since 2018 using our in-house "BrainSaw" serial-section platform. The AMF has three open-source BrainSaw microscopes and about a dozen more have been built by others worldwide (see interactive map on the right). BrainSaw has contributed to over 30 research papers.

Ecosystem

BrainSaw: microscopy systems

We provide CAD models and build instructions for assembling a BrainSaw serial-section two-photon microscope. Systems can be built from scratch, or serial-sectioning functionality added to an existing microscope. We have three BrainSaw systems in-house: NeuroVision, BrainSaw OG, and OrganGrinder.

BakingTray: serial-section acquisition software

BakingTray is modular serial-section acquisition software for MATLAB. It accommodates diverse hardware configurations and acquires images via ScanImage.

Docs

StitchIt: assembling tile scan data into stitched sections

StitchIt runs alongside BakingTray to pre-process data during acquisition, stream the last stitched section to a web page, and initiate stitching automatically when acquisition completes. It includes tools for downsampling and batch-processing image stacks, with operations highly parallelised for speed.

Docs

BrainSaw Channel Chooser

This interactive web tool helps BrainSaw users choose the best wavelength and excitation channels for their fluorophores.

Launch

Analysis: BrainGlobe

Our favoured option for analysis of serial section data is the BrainGlobe suite. These Python-based tools are maintained by the SWC neuroinformatics team. BrainGlobe comprises several software packages to access, analyse, and visualise anatomical data.
