    System specs

    Our slide scanner is a Zeiss Axio Scan. This high-throughput widefield microscope accommodates up to 100 slides, which it images automatically after an interactive setup process. It handles both fluorescence and conventional brightfield. We run this microscope alongside our histology service, but it can also be booked and used independently. You must be trained to have access to it.

    • Fully automated for high throughput
    • Z-drive with 3 mm usable focus range
    • "Magazine" for 100 slides (size 26×76 mm)
    • Precision scanning stage 300×100 mm
    • Light source for bright field: Vis-LED
    • Light source for fluorescence: HXP 120 V (D), for fixed samples

    Important notices

    • All old scan profiles are in C:\OLD_SCAN_PROFILES
    • You can populate your own scan profile directory by copying things into C:\Scan Profiles
    • Shut down the microscope after use.
    • Power off the slide scanner itself using the button.
    • Shut down the PC.
    • The power to the camera is not controlled by the button on the Axio Scan, so you need to turn off the power at the wall.

    Loading slides

    When loading slides in a holder, please make sure that:

    • The cover-slip is hanging over the edge of the slide.
    • The corners of your microscope slide are intact.
    • The slide scanner uses air objectives, so make sure slides are clean and not covered in oil.
    • You do not cover the small round hole in the slide holder (e.g. with tape).

    During loading, if the microscope light flashes red (indicating an error), do not shut down the machine — the internal robotic arm might crash into a slide.

    Usage hints

    • It is possible to define slide names, and even profiles associated with slides, by importing data from an Excel or .csv file.
    • Profiles that lead to very long acquisition times are probably doing so because of focus map parameters that are excessively thorough.
    • Saving the data using Zen's lossy compression (JPEG XR) at 85% has been shown to produce results almost indistinguishable from lossless compression.
    • Avoid auto-exposure with fluorescence imaging — it tends to produce odd results and makes quantification impossible.

    Cameras

    The system contains two high-end imaging cameras and one low-quality overview camera for taking images of the whole slide.

    Bright-field RGB camera

    • Model: 3CCD Hitachi HV-F202SCL for Axio Scan.Z1 (O)
    • Effective number of pixels: 1600 × 1200
    • Pixel size: 4.4 µm × 4.4 µm
    • Effective area: 7.04 mm × 5.28 mm
    • Max frame rate: 30 FPS RGB
    • Dynamic range: 12 bit
    • Sensitivity: 2000 Lux/F5.6

    Monochrome fluorescence camera

    • Model: Orca Flash 4.0 V2 for Axio Scan.Z1 (O)
    • Effective number of pixels: 2048 × 2048
    • Pixel size: 6.5 µm × 6.5 µm
    • Effective area: 13.31 mm × 13.31 mm
    • Spectral range: approx. 250–1000 nm
    • Full frame rate: 100 FPS
    • Dynamic range: 16 bit
    • Quantum efficiency (at 600 nm): > 70%
    • Noise at min EM gain: 1.6 electrons rms

    Note the system is operating below Nyquist: the number of microns per pixel is not less than half the Rayleigh resolution value. This is unlikely to present a problem, given the typical use cases of the system.

    So if you want quick overview scans of the "where is my fluorescence" sort, you should use the 5× with 4×4 binning. That will give 3.5 microns per pixel. For counting somata the 10× with 4×4 binning will work. The 20× will provide brighter images at the cost of longer acquisition times.

    General analysis options

    In addition to the usual suspects, such as Fiji and Icy, you should be aware of:

    • QuPath
    • Zen 3.8
    • cellpose and StarDist for identifying somata

    Registering slices to the atlas

    There are many options, some of which are summarised in this spreadsheet.

    • Allen Brain BIOP Aligner — seems like a good starting point.
    • slicereg — associated with BrainGlobe. Developed in the Kohl lab in collaboration with the SWC.
    • AMaSiNe
    • WholeBrain
    • Geometry Processing of Conventionally Produced Mouse Brain Slices
    • Mapping Histological Slice Sequences to the Allen Mouse Brain Atlas without 3D Reconstruction
    • ABA-J
    • SHARP-Track (by Phillip Shamash, Branco lab)
    • Histology (by Andy Peters, Cortex lab)
